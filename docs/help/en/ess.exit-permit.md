## Overview

Record an exit permit — your employer's permission to leave the country — and have it approved and kept on your file.

This screen does not obtain the permit. You get the permit itself through the usual government process; what you do here is register it with your company so HR has it, it is approved by whoever needs to approve it, and it can be found again when somebody asks for it.

## Exit permit number {#ess.exit-permit.exit-permit-number}
<!-- alias: loc:ExitPermitNumber -->

**What it is.** The reference on the permit itself. Required — the request will not save without it.

**Example.** EP-2026-004512

**Why it matters.** It is what ties the record on your file to the actual document, so copy it exactly as it is printed. A number that does not match is worse than none: it looks checked when it has not been.

## Reason for travel {#ess.exit-permit.travel-reason}
<!-- alias: loc:TravelReason -->

**What it is.** Why you are travelling, in your own words.

**Example.** Family visit — Cairo.

**Why it matters.** It is what your approver reads, and what tells HR later whether the trip was personal or on company business — which is often what decides who pays for it.

## Exit date {#ess.exit-permit.exit-date}
<!-- alias: loc:ExitDate -->

**What it is.** The day you leave the country.

**Example.** 20 September 2026.

**Why it matters.** The permit is valid from this date, and the request drops off your active list once it has passed — so a date typed wrongly can make the record disappear before the trip.

## Start date {#ess.exit-permit.start-date}
<!-- alias: loc:StartDate -->

**What it is.** The day the permit takes effect, which is usually the same as the exit date but can be earlier where the permit is issued to cover a period.

**Example.** 18 September 2026, for a permit covering the week you travel in.

**Why it matters.** It is the date the permit is filed under, so it is what a search finds — and where a permit covers a window rather than a single day, it is the honest start of that window.
